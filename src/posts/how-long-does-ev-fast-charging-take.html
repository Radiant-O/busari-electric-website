Short answer: On a DC fast charger, most electric cars go from about 10% to 80% in 20 to 45 minutes. The exact time depends on your car's maximum charging speed, your battery's temperature and how full it is when you plug in.

Fast charging isn't like filling a gas tank at a steady rate. The speed changes during the session, and knowing why helps you spend less time waiting.

Why the last 20% is slow

Batteries accept power fastest when they're low. As the battery fills, the car slows charging down to protect it. Past about 80%, the speed drops a lot, so going from 80% to 100% can take nearly as long as going from 10% to 80%.

On a road trip, it's usually quicker to charge to 80% and stop again later than to wait for 100%.

What affects charging time

  • Your car's top charging speed. Every EV has a maximum it can accept. A powerful charger can't push more than that.
  • Battery temperature. A cold battery charges slowly. Many EVs warm the battery automatically when you navigate to a fast charger in the car's built-in maps.
  • State of charge. Arriving at 10% gets you the fastest part of the curve. Arriving at 60% doesn't.
  • Battery size. A bigger battery takes longer to fill, even at the same speed.

Tips for a quicker stop

  1. Use your car's navigation to route to the charger so it can prepare the battery.
  2. Arrive with a low battery, around 10% to 20%, when it's safe to do so.
  3. Unplug at around 80% unless you really need more range.
  4. Check charger status in the E-Fill Electric app before you head over.

Fast charging vs. home charging

Fast charging is for when you need range quickly. For everyday driving, a Level 2 home charger fills your battery overnight while you sleep.
