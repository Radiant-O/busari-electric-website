Short answer: Level 1 charging uses a standard 120-volt household outlet and adds roughly 3 to 5 miles of range per hour. Level 2 uses a 240-volt circuit and usually adds about 20 to 40 miles per hour. If you drive more than 30 or 40 miles a day, or want a full battery every morning, Level 2 is the better fit.

Most EV charging happens at home, overnight. The question is how fast you need it to be.

Level 1: the outlet you already have

Level 1 uses a normal 120-volt wall outlet and the portable cord that comes with many EVs. Nothing to install, nothing to buy.

The trade-off is speed. At roughly 3 to 5 miles of range per hour, a 10-hour overnight charge adds about 30 to 50 miles. That's enough for many short commutes, but it can't refill a nearly empty battery overnight.

Good for: plug-in hybrids, short daily drives, and drivers who can top up at a fast charger now and then.

Level 2: faster charging at home

Level 2 runs on 240 volts, the same kind of circuit a clothes dryer or electric range uses. A wall-mounted Level 2 charger typically adds about 20 to 40 miles of range per hour, depending on the charger and your car. Most drivers wake up to a full battery.

It needs a dedicated 240-volt circuit installed by a licensed electrician. Depending on your home, that may mean upgrading your electrical panel, and your city may require a permit.

Good for: daily commuters, households with two EVs, and anyone who wants to skip public charging on most days.

Questions to ask before you choose

  • How many miles do you drive on a normal day?
  • Where do you park: a garage, a driveway or the street?
  • How old is your electrical panel, and does it have room for a new 240-volt circuit?
  • Do you plan to own a second EV in the next few years?

Common questions

Can I use a regular outlet to charge my EV?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes. That's Level 1 charging. It uses a standard 120-volt outlet and adds roughly 3 to 5 miles of range per hour.

Do I need an electrician for a Level 2 charger?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes. A Level 2 charger needs a dedicated 240-volt circuit, which should be installed by a licensed electrician. Some homes also need a panel upgrade or a permit.

Is Level 2 the same as fast charging?{{icon:chevron-down}}

No. Fast charging, also called DC fast charging, is much quicker and is found at public stations like ours. Level 2 is the fastest option most homes can have.
