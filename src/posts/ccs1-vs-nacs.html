Short answer: NACS is the plug Tesla created, now standardized as SAE J3400. CCS1 is the plug most other US electric cars have used for years. Many automakers are moving to NACS, so newer cars from Ford, GM, Hyundai, Kia and others may have a NACS port instead of CCS1. Our Arlington station has both cables.

Pull up to a fast charger and you'll usually see two different plugs hanging on it. Picking the right one is easy once you know what each looks like.

What is CCS1?

CCS1 stands for Combined Charging System, type 1. It's the larger plug with a round section on top and two big pins underneath. For years it was the fast charging standard for nearly every non-Tesla EV in North America: the Ford Mustang Mach-E, Chevrolet Bolt, Hyundai Ioniq 5, Kia EV6, Volkswagen ID.4, BMW i4 and many more.

What is NACS?

NACS stands for North American Charging Standard. Tesla designed it, and SAE International standardized it as J3400. The plug is noticeably smaller and slimmer than CCS1, and the same port handles both home charging and fast charging.

Every Tesla sold in the US uses NACS. Starting with some 2025 models, other automakers began building cars with NACS ports too, and more are following.

How to check which port your car has

  • Look at the port. A small, slim port with no separate lower section is NACS. A port with a round top and a separate two-pin section below is CCS1.
  • Check your owner's manual or your automaker's app.
  • Search your model and year plus "charging port." Brands switched at different times, so the year matters.

Do I need an adapter?

Only when the station doesn't have a cable for your port. A CCS1 car at a NACS-only station needs a NACS-to-CCS1 adapter, and a NACS car at a CCS1-only station needs the reverse. Use adapters approved by your automaker.

At our station on South Collins Street you don't need one. Just pick the cable that fits.

What about CHAdeMO?

CHAdeMO is an older fast charging plug used mainly by the Nissan Leaf and a few early EVs. It's being phased out in the US, and our station doesn't have a CHAdeMO cable.

Common questions

Is NACS the same as a Tesla plug?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes. NACS is the plug Tesla designed. It was opened to other automakers and standardized by SAE as J3400.

Can a CCS1 car charge at a NACS charger?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes, with an approved NACS-to-CCS1 adapter, as long as the charger network allows your car. At BusterElectric you don't need one, because we have both CCS1 and NACS cables.

Is NACS faster than CCS1?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Not by itself. Charging speed depends on the charger's power and what your car can accept, not on the shape of the plug.
