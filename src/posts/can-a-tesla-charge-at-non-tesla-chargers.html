Short answer: Yes. If the station has a NACS cable, a Tesla plugs straight in. If it only has CCS1 cables, you need a CCS1-to-NACS adapter. BusterElectric in Arlington has NACS cables, so no adapter is needed, and you pay with a card or the E-Fill Electric app.

Superchargers aren't the only place a Tesla can charge. Plenty of other networks work with Teslas too.

Stations with a NACS cable

NACS is the plug Tesla designed, so a station with a NACS cable works like a Supercharger as far as the plug goes. Park, plug in, and start the session. Our station on South Collins Street has NACS cables on its fast chargers.

Stations with only CCS1 cables

Here you need a CCS1-to-NACS adapter, which Tesla sells. Your car also has to support CCS charging. Most recent Teslas do, but some older ones need an upgrade, so check in your car's software info or with Tesla before you rely on one.

How payment works

At a non-Tesla station you don't pay through the Tesla app. At BusterElectric, you can:

  • Tap or insert a credit or debit card on the charger, or
  • Start and pay in the E-Fill Electric app.

The price shows on the charger screen and in the app before you start. More on pricing and payment.

Will it charge as fast as a Supercharger?

That depends on the charger's power and on what your car can take. The plug type doesn't limit speed. Battery temperature and charge level matter more, which we cover in how long fast charging takes.
