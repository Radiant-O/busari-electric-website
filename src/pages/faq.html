Charging basics

What is the difference between DC fast charging and Level 2?{{icon:chevron-down}}

DC fast chargers supply direct current straight to your battery at up to {{stations.maxKw}} kW, adding hundreds of miles of range in under an hour. They are best for road trips and quick stops. Level 2 chargers supply alternating current at up to 19 kW and add about 20 to 60 miles of range per hour. They are best when you are parked for a few hours, such as at work, a hotel or while shopping.

How long does it take to charge an electric car?{{icon:chevron-down}}

On a DC fast charger, most electric cars go from 10% to 80% in about 20 to 40 minutes. On a Level 2 charger, a full charge usually takes 4 to 10 hours. Time depends on your car's maximum charging speed, battery size, battery temperature and starting charge.

Why does charging slow down after 80%?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Your car reduces charging speed as the battery fills to protect it. Charging from 80% to 100% can take as long as charging from 10% to 80%. On road trips, it is usually faster to charge to about 80% and stop again later.

Does cold weather affect fast charging?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes. A cold battery accepts power more slowly. If your car has battery preconditioning, set the Busari Electric station as your navigation destination so the car warms the battery on the way and charges faster when you arrive.

Plugs and cars

Which plug does my electric car use?{{icon:chevron-down}}
  • CCS1: the fast-charging plug on most non-Tesla EVs sold in the US, including Ford, Chevrolet, Hyundai, Kia, Volkswagen, BMW and Rivian models.
  • NACS (SAE J3400): the plug on Tesla cars and a growing number of 2025 and newer models from other brands.
  • J1772: the Level 2 plug used by nearly every EV in the US. Tesla cars use their J1772 adapter.

Busari Electric DC fast chargers have both CCS1 and NACS cables, so nearly every EV can charge without an adapter.

Can I charge a Tesla at Busari Electric?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes. Use the NACS cable on our DC fast chargers. At Level 2 chargers, use the J1772 adapter that came with your Tesla.

Can I charge a plug-in hybrid?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes, at our Level 2 chargers. Most plug-in hybrids cannot use DC fast chargers, so check your owner's manual before choosing a DC fast cable.

What is an idle fee?{{icon:chevron-down}}

An idle fee of {{price.idlePerMinute}} per minute applies if your car stays plugged in more than {{price.idleGraceMinutes}} minutes after charging finishes. It keeps chargers free for the next driver. You get a text before the fee starts if you gave us your phone number.

Why is there a pending charge on my card?{{icon:chevron-down}}

When you start a session, your bank may place a temporary hold on your card. After the session, the hold is replaced by the actual amount, usually within a few business days depending on your bank.

At the station

What should I do if a charger is not working?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Try another charger at the station first. Then contact us at {{site.email}} with the station name and the charger number printed on the unit. We can often restart a charger remotely, and we send a technician when it needs repair.

Can I leave my car while it charges?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes. The cable locks to your car while charging. Many stations are next to restrooms, food and coffee, listed on each station in the map. Come back within {{price.idleGraceMinutes}} minutes of charging finishing to avoid idle fees.

Are Busari Electric stations accessible?{{icon:chevron-down}}

New Busari Electric stations are designed to meet the Americans with Disabilities Act, with an accessible parking space, reachable payment reader and cable at each site. If you have trouble using any station, contact us so we can fix it.

Is it safe to charge in the rain?{{icon:chevron-down}}

Yes. EV chargers and car charging ports are sealed and built to work in rain and snow. Power only flows after the plug and car confirm a secure connection.
